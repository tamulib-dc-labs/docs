Products and Product Ownership¶

About¶

The Product Owner operates at the center of the product ecosystem, ensuring that stakeholder needs are understood, customer input is effectively translated, and development efforts are aligned with strategic objectives. They hold decision authority while maintaining continuous collaboration across institutional and technical boundaries.

This document lays out product ownership for products in the Digital Collections unit. It describes what is expected of the product owner, whom they represent, and what is necessary to successfully be a product owner for a service or application.

If you want to weigh in on the direction of a product, contact its Product Owner. To propose a new digital project, start with the DPMT process rather than contacting a Product Owner directly. See Partners and Stakeholders for the full list of internal and external groups we work with.

Terminology¶

  • Product: A service, application, or platform that Digital Collections operates, develops, or relies on to deliver its work. A product has a Product Owner, at least one instance, and stakeholders.

  • Stakeholder: Any individual or group impacted by the product, directly or indirectly. This includes end users, institutional units, and external parties whose work or outcomes are affected.

  • Customer: A subset of stakeholders who are actively engaged in the product development process. Customers interact directly with the Product Owner, contribute requirements, provide feedback, and influence product direction.

  • Product Owner: The single person accountable for a product’s direction, priorities, and health. A product has exactly one Product Owner at a time, even when many people contribute to it.

Roles of Product Owner¶

  • Product Stewardship (Governance & Long-Term Planning): Ensure the long-term health, sustainability, and strategic alignment of the product. Establish and maintain governance structures, guide long-term product direction, and ensure continuity across development cycles. Balance immediate delivery needs with future viability, including technical sustainability, institutional alignment, and evolving stakeholder needs.

  • Stakeholder Management: Maintain awareness of all stakeholder groups, ensure clear stakeholder identification and definition, and understand their needs and their impact on the product, and the product’s impact on each stakeholder group.

  • Requirements Gathering: Actively engage customers to identify needs, anticipate requirements, and refine problem spaces.

  • Backlog Curation: Own and prioritize the product backlog in collaboration with the development team, balancing competing priorities while maintaining a coherent product vision.

  • Champion / Approval Authority: Serve as the final decision-maker for product direction, scope, and acceptance of work. Act as the primary advocate for the product, ensuring it receives appropriate development time, resources, and organizational support. Represent the product in prioritization discussions, defend its value, and ensure its needs are clearly articulated.

  • Sprint Participation: Engage in sprint planning, reviews, and ongoing team interaction to ensure alignment and delivery quality.

Decision Authority¶

The Product Owner is the final decision-maker for the following, in consultation with customers and the development team:

  • What gets built, fixed, or deferred, and in what order

  • Whether completed work is accepted

  • Whether the product is upgraded, migrated, or retired

  • Who counts as a customer and how their feedback is gathered

Decisions that cross product boundaries (for example, a change in Fedora that affects CAP, SAGE, and Avalon) should be raised with every affected Product Owner and, where they cannot agree, with the Director of Digital Collections. See People.

What Every Product Owner Should Be Able to Do¶

Regardless of the product, a Product Owner should be able to:

  • Explain what the product does, who uses it, and why the Libraries run it

  • Name the product’s dependencies (see “Supports” and “Supported By” below) and what breaks if one of them goes down

  • Describe each instance (Dev, Pre, Prod) and how a change moves between them

  • Find the code, configuration, and documentation for the product

  • Identify the upstream community, if there is one, and how to reach it

  • Keep a current list of customers and known stakeholders

  • Maintain a backlog, even if that backlog is short

  • Say what the plan is for the product’s future, including when it will be retired

Becoming or Handing Off a Product Owner¶

When product ownership changes, the outgoing and incoming owners should work through the following together before the change is recorded on this page:

  1. Review the product’s entry on this page and update anything that is out of date.

  2. Walk through every instance, including how to log in, where logs live, and how to deploy.

  3. Transfer access: repositories, upstream community accounts and mailing lists or Slack channels, and any vendor or consortium contacts.

  4. Introduce the new owner to customers and to Technology Services contacts, where applicable.

  5. Hand over the backlog, open issues, and any commitments made to stakeholders.

  6. Update the “Current Product Owner” line and notify the team.

How to Read a Product Entry¶

Each product below uses the same fields where they apply.

  • Current Product Owner: The person accountable for the product.

  • Repository: Where the code or configuration lives.

  • Development Support from Technology Services: Whether Technology Services (TS) staff develop the product.

    • Yes: TS provides development support.

    • Yes (Third Party — Contribute): TS supports a third-party product and contributes changes back to it.

    • No (Third Party — Consume): The product is third-party and we use it as released.

    • No (DevOps and Deployment only): TS deploys and hosts the product but does not develop it.

    • No (Not listed as a Technology Services product): TS does not formally list or support the product.

  • Campus Category: Common products are widely used software that other institutions also run. Unique products are built at Texas A&M for needs specific to us.

  • Dependencies: Each product has a Dependencies section with two lines. Depends on (Supported By) lists the products this product needs in order to work. Used by (Supports) lists the products that need this one. Before changing, upgrading, or retiring a product, check its “Used by” line and talk to those Product Owners.

  • Instances and Clusters: The environments in which the product runs.

  • PODS and Containers: The Kubernetes pods and containers that make up a running instance.

  • Product Owner Expectations: What is asked of this product’s owner beyond the general expectations above.

  • Future Plans: Planned retirement, migration, or major change.

Archipelago¶

About¶

Archipelago Commons is an open-source digital repository and digital asset management (DAM) platform built on top of Drupal and developed by the Metropolitan New York Library Council. It’s designed primarily for the GLAM community to manage, preserve, and provide access to digital collections. As of 2026, it is the primary solution for cultural heritage collections at Texas A&M University Libraries.

  • Current Product Owner: Mark Baggett

  • Repositories:

    • https://github.com/esmero

    • https://github.com/cantaloupe-project/cantaloupe

  • Development Support from Technology Services: No (DevOps and Deployment only)

Dependencies¶

  • Depends on (Supported By): Not yet recorded

  • Used by (Supports): Not yet recorded

Instances and Clusters¶

  • Dev

  • Pre

  • Prod

PODS and Containers¶

  • esmero-cantaloupe: Used as image server in each cluster

  • esmero-minio: Used as storage instance in dev and pre. Not used in prod.

  • esmero-nlp: Used for complex processing including in the OCR process

  • esmero-php: Used as Drupal, PHP, and Drush layer.

  • esmero-solr: Solr instance

  • esmero-web: Web server

Product Owner Expectations¶

  • Serves as technical expert of platform within the Libraries

  • Monitors Archipelago community development and contributes issues and other problems as necessary

  • Liaises with Archipelago community

  • Listens to feedback from library stakeholders and brings problems or solutions to Archipelago community or digital collections team

  • Leads troubleshooting of problems with platform

  • Monitors Archipelago security for needed fixes

  • Ensures the application is up to date, functioning, and secure

  • Monitors logs to ensure it is is functioning as expected

Related Documentation¶

See the applications/archipelago section of this documentation for workflows and troubleshooting.

Fedora¶

About¶

Fedora is a digital repository that uses linked data (RDF). It is used as the assets management system for SAGE and Spotlight collections. The same instance is also used for Avalon Media System, although it functions very differently. Specifically, Avalon only uses Fedora for asset management. It does not use Fedora’s Solr or Fuseki instance which can be problematic for SAGE collections.

Also, TAMU has its own specific flavor of RDF based loosely around PCDM.

  • Current Product Owner: Mark Baggett

  • Development Support from Technology Services: No (Third Party — Consume)

  • Campus Category: Common

Dependencies¶

  • Depends on (Supported By): CAP

  • Used by (Supports): Avalon, IIIF Service, SAGE, Spotlight

Product Owner Expectations¶

  • Serves as technical expert of the platform and of TAMU’s PCDM-based RDF model

  • Coordinates changes that affect more than one dependent product (Avalon, IIIF Service, SAGE, Spotlight)

  • Leads troubleshooting of problems with platform

Future Plans¶

A Fedora migration is planned. CAP and Cantaloupe will be spun down once it is complete.
