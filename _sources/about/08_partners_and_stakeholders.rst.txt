=========================
Partners and Stakeholders
=========================

Digital Collections does not operate in isolation. Our systems, projects, and services depend on
ongoing collaboration with other units across the Libraries and University, as well as external
partners in the broader scholarly communication and cultural heritage community. This page identifies
who those partners are and where to learn more about how we work with them.

------------------------------------
Internal Library and Campus Partners
------------------------------------

* **Cushing Memorial Library and Archives (Special Collections)** — the source of most of our cultural
  heritage collections: rare books, oral histories, manuscripts, correspondence, photographs, maps, and
  newspapers. See :doc:`04_cultral_heritage` for collection-level detail.
* **Center for Digital Humanities Research (CoDHR)** — submits Digital Humanities project requests that
  are routed through the DPMT service track for digitization and technical support on materials not
  owned by the Libraries or University.
* **Technology Services** — a campus partner on infrastructure and deposit processes for some of our
  legacy systems. We interact with them through the `help desk <https://helpdesk.library.tamu.edu/default_kace.php>`_
  and on Slack.
* **Cataloging and Metadata**: is responsible for all descriptive metadata for all projects.
* **Texas A&M High Performance Research Computing (HPRC)** — provides the computational infrastructure
  behind some of our AI/ML initiatives, including metadata enrichment and captioning pipelines.
* **Academic departments and colleges** — content and subject-matter partners for specific collections
  and projects, such as the College of Medicine and the Department of Hispanic Studies. See
  :doc:`04_cultral_heritage` for examples.
* **Digital Project Management Team (DPMT)** — the cross-functional team that reviews and prioritizes
  incoming digital project requests. Any stakeholder proposing a new digital collection or project
  works with DPMT first; see the DPMT process documentation for how project intake works.
* **Project stakeholders and curators** — the individuals and departments who submit project proposals
  ("IDEA Documents"), select and describe materials, and make access-rights decisions for their
  collections.

-----------------
External Partners
-----------------

* **Texas Digital Library (TDL)** — a consortium we partner with to host and support open-access
  journal publishing through Open Journal Systems (OJS). See `tdl.org <https://www.tdl.org/>`_.
* **Texas A&M AgriLife and the Texas Engineering Extension Service (TEEX)** — University partners whose
  scholarly output is collected and preserved in OAKTrust alongside the University's own.
* **EZID / California Digital Library** — the persistent identifier service we use to mint and manage
  ARKs. See `ezid.cdlib.org <https://ezid.cdlib.org/>`_.
* **Crossref**: the persistent identifier service we use to mint and manage DOIS. See `crossref <https://crossref.org>`_.
* **The DSpace community (Lyrasis, 4Science, and other contributors)** — the open-source community
  behind DSpace, the platform underlying OAKTrust. We currently use vanilla DSPACE.
* **The Archipelago community (Metropolitan New York Library Council and the broader GLAM community)**
  — the open-source community behind Archipelago Commons, our digital asset management platform for
  cultural heritage materials. See :doc:`05_products_and_product_ownership` for more on this
  relationship.
* **IIIF Constortium**: a set of open standards for delivering high-quality, attributed digital objects online at scale.
  It’s also an international community developing and implementing the IIIF APIs. IIIF is backed by a consortium of
  leading cultural institutions, of which we are one.  See `iiif <https://iiif.io>`_
* **AllMaps**: makes it easier and more inspiring to curate, georeference and explore collections of digitized maps. We
  are a contributing institution. See `allmaps <https://allmaps.org>`_
* **Fromthepage**:  a crowdsourcing platform for archives and libraries where volunteers transcribe, index, and
  describe historic documents. We are a participating member. See `fromthepage <https://fromthepage.com/>`_

---------------------
Working With Our Team
---------------------

* To propose a new digital project, start with the DPMT intake process rather than contacting Digital
  Collections staff directly — see the DPMT process documentation and :doc:`07_onboarding_checklist`
  for where that fits into a new team member's first week.
* To weigh in on the direction of a specific application or platform, reach out to that system's
  Product Owner. See :doc:`05_products_and_product_ownership`.
* For unfamiliar terms or system names used above, see :doc:`06_glossary`.
