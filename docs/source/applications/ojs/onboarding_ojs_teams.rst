===================================
1-Hour Journal Onboarding Checklist
===================================

*Open Access Journal Setup in OJS · Facilitated by Mark Baggett*

This checklist covers what I normally use to run a 1-hour onboarding session with a new open access
journal team getting started in OJS. Each hands-on section includes numbered
steps, an example scenario, and a quick checklist recap.

There is a `Slideshow <https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1ES_aneijRbLWLYUg0Py24kMvAjRn41th/edit?slide=id.p1#slide=id.p1>`_
and `Google Doc <https://docs.google.com/document/d/1bUlYS7w5fBUCujWE2AUkJ1xvBgQWih06/edit?usp=sharing&ouid=105546367335888507442&rtpof=true&sd=true>`_ also available.

Exemplar Journal: Texas Water Journal
======================================

When the team is deciding how to configure their own policies, use the
`Texas Water Journal <https://twj-ojs-tdl.tdl.org/twj/index>`_ as a working,
real-world example — it is a well-run, well-indexed OA journal built on OJS
with clearly documented process and policy pages.

.. list-table:: Texas Water Journal's Approach
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 25 75

   * - Policy Area
     - Texas Water Journal's Approach
   * - OA model
     - Diamond/platinum open access — no author fees (APCs) and no reader or
       subscription fees.
   * - License
     - CC BY 4.0. Authors retain copyright; the journal receives right of
       first publication.
   * - Peer review
     - Blind review (reviewer sees the author's identity; author does not
       see the reviewer's). Minimum of three reviewers per article;
       reviewers typically get about one month.
   * - Editorial structure
     - Editor-in-chief pre-screens each submission, then assigns it to a
       section/article editor who selects reviewers and forwards a
       recommendation.
   * - Sections
     - Articles, Program Review, and Legal Review, plus invitation-only
       Commentaries and Book Reviews.
   * - Publication schedule
     - One primary annual issue, but articles are posted continuously as
       they're finished rather than held for a single release; occasional
       special issues.
   * - Indexing & recognition
     - Google Scholar, Scopus, and DOAJ — holds the DOAJ Seal for
       demonstrated best practice in OA publishing.
   * - Archiving
     - Preserved long-term via the PKP Preservation Network (PKP PN).
   * - Governance
     - Independent 501(c)(3) nonprofit governed by a volunteer board;
       published in partnership with three universities (Texas A&M, UT
       Austin, and Texas Tech).

*Full policy language:* `texaswaterjournal.org/twj/about <https://twj-ojs-tdl.tdl.org/twj/about>`_

Before the Session
===================

Send this checklist to the journal team ahead of time and confirm the
following are ready:

- ☐ OJS account credentials created for the Journal Manager
- ☐ Journal title, abbreviation, and ISSN (if assigned) on hand
- ☐ Logo file and short journal description ready to upload
- ☐ List of editorial team members (names, emails, intended roles)
- ☐ Decision on default license (e.g., CC BY 4.0) and OA policy language
- ☐ A sample/test manuscript file for the live demo submission

1. Journal Setup Basics — 10 min
==================================

How To
------

#. Log in as Journal Manager and go to **Settings > Journal > Masthead**.
#. Enter the journal title and abbreviation, then click Save.
#. Go to **Settings > Journal > Identity** to add the ISSN(s), if assigned.
#. Go to **Settings > Website > Setup** to select the primary language and
   add any additional submission languages.
#. Go to **Settings > Distribution > License** to set the default open
   access license (e.g., CC BY 4.0) and enter the OA policy statement.
#. Back in Masthead/Setup, upload the journal logo and add a short
   description and contact email.

.. note::

   **Example scenario:** The team is launching the Journal of Applied
   Ecology Studies. Walk through entering "Journal of Applied Ecology
   Studies" as the title and "JAES" as the abbreviation, set the primary
   language to English, and set the license to CC BY 4.0 with an OA
   statement such as: "All content is freely available without charge
   under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 license."

*Real-world model: Texas Water Journal is diamond OA (no author or reader
fees) and licensed CC BY 4.0 — see* `texaswaterjournal.org/twj/about
<https://twj-ojs-tdl.tdl.org/twj/about>`__ *for their exact policy wording.*

Quick Checklist
----------------

- ☐ Confirm journal title, abbreviation, and ISSN (print & online, if applicable)
- ☐ Set primary language and any additional submission languages
- ☐ Configure open access policy statement and default license (e.g., CC BY 4.0)
- ☐ Set journal description, contact info, and upload logo

2. Roles & Users — 10 min
===========================

Users & Roles
--------------

- ☐ Review core roles and what each one does (see reference table below)
- ☐ Add/invite editorial team members live
- ☐ Confirm at least one Journal Manager and one Editor are assigned
- ☐ Explain how reviewers are added (individually or via bulk CSV import)

Role Reference: Who Does What
-------------------------------

Use this as a quick reference when explaining who fills each role and what
they're responsible for:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 20 80

   * - Role
     - What They Do
   * - Journal Manager
     - Owns overall journal setup: configures settings, manages user
       accounts, assigns roles, and oversees the journal's day-to-day
       operation. Usually the primary contact and first account created.
   * - Editor
     - Oversees the full editorial process across the journal. Assigns
       incoming submissions to section editors, tracks progress, and makes
       final acceptance/rejection decisions.
   * - Section Editor
     - Manages submissions within a specific section (e.g., Articles or
       Reviews). Assigns reviewers, monitors the review process, and
       recommends a decision to the editor.
   * - Reviewer
     - Evaluates assigned manuscripts for quality, rigor, and fit, then
       submits comments and a recommendation (accept, revise, reject) to
       the editor. Does not have broader journal access.
   * - Author
     - Submits manuscripts through the online system, responds to reviewer
       and editor feedback, and revises drafts as requested. Can track
       their submission's status at each stage.
   * - Copyeditor
     - Edits accepted manuscripts for grammar, clarity, consistency, and
       journal style before layout. Works directly with the author on
       final wording.
   * - Layout Editor
     - Converts copyedited manuscripts into publication-ready galleys
       (PDF, HTML, XML, etc.), applying the journal's formatting and
       design standards.
   * - Proofreader
     - Does a final quality check on galleys — catching typos, formatting
       errors, or layout issues — before the issue is published.

.. note::

   One person can hold multiple roles (e.g., a small journal's Editor may
   also serve as Journal Manager). Roles can be assigned per-user in
   **Users & Roles > Add User**.

3. Sections & Submission Setup — 10 min
=========================================

How To
------

#. Go to **Settings > Journal > Sections**.
#. Click "Add Section." Enter a section title (e.g., Articles) and
   abbreviation (e.g., ART), then Save.
#. Repeat for each additional section the journal needs.
#. Go to **Settings > Journal > Submissions** to define the checklist
   authors must confirm before submitting.
#. On the same tab, paste or write the author guidelines.
#. If the journal needs extra metadata, enable fields (e.g., keywords,
   funding info) under **Settings > Journal > Submissions > Metadata**.

.. note::

   **Example scenario:** This journal wants three sections: Articles, Case
   Reports, and Editorials. Create each section live with the team, using
   abbreviations ART, CR, and ED. Then add a submission checklist item such
   as "This submission has not been previously published" and turn on the
   Keywords metadata field so authors can tag their work.

*Real-world model: Texas Water Journal uses three main sections — Articles,
Program Review, and Legal Review — plus invitation-only Commentaries and
Book Reviews.*

Quick Checklist
----------------

- ☐ Create journal sections (e.g., Articles, Reviews, Editorials) with abbreviations
- ☐ Set the submission checklist authors must confirm
- ☐ Draft or paste in author guidelines
- ☐ Enable any additional metadata fields needed (keywords, funding info, etc.)

4. Editorial Workflow Walkthrough — 15 min
=============================================

How To
------

#. Log in as (or switch to) an Author account and go to **Submissions >
   New Submission**.
#. Upload a test manuscript file and complete the submission metadata
   (title, abstract, authors).
#. Switch to the Editor role. Under **Submissions > Active**, open the new
   submission and assign it to a reviewer in the Review stage.
#. Open the reviewer assignment and show the review form fields
   (recommendation, comments to editor/author).
#. As the editor, record a review decision (e.g., Accept) and move the
   submission to Copyediting.
#. In Copyediting, show where copyedited files are uploaded and how the
   author reviews and approves them.
#. Move the submission to Production and show where layout/galley files
   are uploaded.

.. note::

   **Example scenario:** Submit a mock manuscript titled "A Test
   Submission for JAES Onboarding" as the author. Assign it to a reviewer
   (a second team member or a demo reviewer account) and complete a sample
   review recommending "Accept." Then walk it through copyediting and
   production together so the team sees a submission move start to finish.

Peer Review Options
---------------------

OJS supports several review models. Configure the default under
**Settings > Workflow > Review** — worth a one-line mention during the
walkthrough rather than a deep dive in a 1-hour session:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 20 80

   * - Model
     - How It Works
   * - Open Review
     - Reviewer and author identities are visible to each other. Builds
       transparency; common in smaller or close-knit fields.
   * - Blind Review
     - The reviewer sees the author's identity, but the author does not
       see the reviewer's. OJS's most common default. Texas Water Journal
       uses this model, with a minimum of three reviewers per article and
       about a one-month review window.
   * - Double-Blind Review
     - Neither party sees the other's identity. Standard for most
       traditional academic journals; requires authors to submit an
       anonymized manuscript file.
   * - No Formal Review
     - The editor records a recommendation directly without a structured
       reviewer role. Used by some invitation-only or editorially reviewed
       journals.

*Other settings in the same tab: number of reviewers required per
submission, review due dates, and whether to use OJS's default review form
or a custom one.*

Quick Checklist
----------------

- ☐ Submission — author uploads files and metadata
- ☐ Review — editor assigns a reviewer; show the review form
- ☐ Confirm the journal's review model (open/blind/double-blind) and where
  it's configured (Settings > Workflow > Review)
- ☐ Copyediting — manuscript is copyedited and author reviews changes
- ☐ Production — layout, galleys, and final proof are prepared

5. Publishing an Issue — 10 min
=================================

How To
------

#. Go to **Issues > Future Issues > Create Issue**.
#. Enter the volume number, issue number, year, and optional issue title,
   then click Save.
#. Return to the mock submission (now in Production) and click "Schedule
   For Publication."
#. Select the issue you just created and confirm the section it belongs
   to.
#. Go back to **Issues > Future Issues**, open the issue, and
   confirm/reorder the table of contents.
#. Click "Publish Issue" to make it live.

.. note::

   **Example scenario:** Create Volume 1, Issue 1 (2026) titled "Inaugural
   Issue." Schedule the mock article from the workflow demo into that
   issue, confirm it appears correctly in the table of contents, and
   publish it together so the team sees their first issue go live.

*Real-world model: Texas Water Journal publishes one primary issue per
year but posts articles continuously as they're finished, rather than
holding them for a single release — worth raising if the team wants faster
time-to-publication.*

Quick Checklist
----------------

- ☐ Create a volume/issue (or explain the continuous/rolling model if used instead)
- ☐ Schedule the completed mock article into the new issue
- ☐ Arrange table-of-contents order and confirm section placement
- ☐ Publish the issue
