Teaching the OJS 3.5 Editorial Workflow (60 min)¶

Session at a glance¶

One pre-staged test submission carries the whole hour: attendees watch it move from Submission to Published, then practice on scenarios.

Audience: journal editors and section editors (copyeditors and layout editors welcome). Goal: by the end, each attendee can find their submissions on the 3.5 dashboard, take a submission through one review round, and know where copyediting and production happen. Out of scope: journal settings, user management beyond invitations, and theming.

Before the session¶

Start prep at least a week out: in 3.5, Journal Editors can no longer assign roles without sending an invitation the user must accept, so dummy accounts depend on someone clicking email links. Only Site Administrators (TDL, for our instance) can edit user details directly.

1. Get a sandbox journal. Ask TDL for a test journal on the 3.5 instance, or use a real journal with clearly labeled TEST submissions you delete afterward. Confirm the instance sends email, since invitations depend on it.

2. Create the cast. Use mailboxes you control (shared or departmental addresses work well). For each, go to Settings > Users & Roles > Invite to a Role, search to confirm no existing account, enter the name and role, then Save and Continue > Invite user to the role. Accept each invite from its mailbox.

3. Pre-stage four submissions (as Ana Author, using Start a New Submission), each with an obviously fake title:

  1. “TEST A” left in the Submission stage, unassigned (you assign it live).

  2. “TEST B” sent to Review with Rev One and Rev Two assigned; complete Rev One’s review ahead of time so there is something to read.

  3. “TEST C” in Copyediting, with a draft file and a Copyeditor assigned.

  4. “TEST D” in Production, with a PDF ready to upload as a galley, plus a future issue created to schedule it into.

4. Set up the screen.

  • ☐ Two browser profiles: one logged in as Ed Itor, one as Ana Author or a reviewer, to show both sides of a handoff. As a Journal Manager you can also use Login As from the Participants menu, but every action taken that way is logged.

  • ☐ Zoom the browser to 125% so the dashboard reads on a projector.

  • ☐ Start the recording.

  • ☐ Open the PKP editorial workflow guide in a tab for the wrap-up.

Block 1 (0–10 min): Orientation¶

The point to land: four stages, and the 3.5 dashboard is a set of filtered views over them. Navigation changed in 3.5; the stages did not.

  1. Draw the lifecycle (whiteboard or one slide): Submission → Review → Copyediting → Production → Scheduled → Published, with Declined branching off Submission and Review. Say once: any stage can be skipped (for example, Accept and Skip Review).

  2. Log in as Ed Itor and open the Dashboard from the user menu. It opens on Assigned to Me.

  3. Walk the columns: ID, Submission, Stage, Days (time in the current stage), Editorial Activity, Actions (View).

  4. Point out the stage dots. Name each status: Incomplete, Submission, Review (with round number), Copyediting, Production, Scheduled for Publication, Published, Declined.

  5. Click through the views in the left menu and watch the counts. Mention that Needs Editor and Declined are only visible at Journal Manager level; Section Editors see only their own declines.

  6. Search and filter. Search for “TEST” to show all four submissions, then open Filters and show Sections and Days Since Last Activity. Tip for editors: Days Since Last Activity is the fastest way to find stalled items.

  7. Show the bell icon (Tasks), top right. Mention the weekly email summary of items needing action.

  8. Open TEST A with View and name the three columns of the submission record:

    • Left: the Workflow menu (the four stages) and the Publication menu (title, metadata, galleys, issue).

    • Center: stage status, Files, and Discussions.

    • Right: action buttons (decisions) and Participants.

    • Top right: Activity Log (with editor-only Notes) and Library.

Tip

Say this: “If you get lost, the left menu tells you where you are, the right column tells you what you can do.”

3.4 users will ask: Workflow and Publication used to be tabs across the top; they now sit in the left menu. Deleting Incomplete submissions is new (three dots next to Filter > Delete Incomplete Submissions).

Block 2 (10–20 min): Submission stage¶

The point to land: nothing happens until an editor is a participant; then three buttons decide the submission’s path.

Demo on TEST A (unassigned):

  1. Open TEST A. Note the right column has no decision buttons yet, because no editor is assigned.

  2. Explain automatic assignment in one breath: if the journal has one editor, or editors assigned to the section or category, OJS assigns and notifies them automatically. Unassigned items show in the Needs Editor view.

  3. In Participants, click Assign. Search by role, choose Section Editor, and point out the count of submissions already assigned to each person (useful for balancing workload).

  4. Select Sam Section. Show the two choices that matter:

    • Can record a decision vs. can only recommend one.

    • Can or cannot edit publication details.

  5. Keep the default email template, click OK. Sam now appears in Participants.

  6. Point out that a Pre-Review Discussion was created automatically.

  7. Add a discussion: click Add discussion, pick Ana Author as participant, choose the generic Discussion template, write “Please confirm the figures are your own,” and click OK.

  8. Switch to the author browser and show Ana sees the discussion. Reply as Ana with Add Message.

  9. Back as Ed Itor, show the three action buttons and explain each:

    • Send to Review: moves to the Review stage; you choose which files reviewers get.

    • Accept and Skip Review: jumps straight to Copyediting (editorials, book reviews).

    • Decline Submission: desk reject; reversible with Revert Decline.

  10. Click Send to Review, check the files reviewers should see, and click OK. TEST A’s stage dot changes on the dashboard.

Tip

Say this: “Discussions are the paper trail. Replying to the notification email from your inbox does not log anything in OJS. Reply in the Discussions panel.”

Block 3 (20–35 min): Review stage¶

The point to land: assign reviewers, watch the icons, confirm each review, then record one decision. This block gets the most time because it is where editors get stuck.

Part 1, assign a reviewer (live, on TEST A, now in Review):

  1. On the dashboard, open the Needs reviewers view. TEST A is there with an Assign Reviewers button in Editorial Activity.

  2. Click it. Walk the reviewer list: affiliation, active reviews, days since last assigned. Mention that author-suggested reviewers appear here too (new in 3.5).

  3. Click the blue arrow beside a reviewer to show their history and interests.

  4. Point to the bottom of the list: Create New Reviewer (no account yet) and Enroll Existing User (has an account, not a reviewer).

  5. Select Reviewer on Rev One. In the request window, set the Response Due Date and Review Due Date, open Files to be Reviewed, and confirm the review type.

  6. Anonymity check: if the review is Anonymous Reviewer/Anonymous Author, select only anonymized files. OJS hides names in the system, but not inside the Word file or its document properties.

  7. Click Add Reviewer.

Part 2, read the dashboard icons (switch to TEST B):

  1. Open the Awaiting reviews and Reviews submitted views and read TEST B’s Editorial Activity icons aloud.

Part 3, read and confirm a review (TEST B):

  1. In the Reviewers panel, click Read Review beside Rev One. Show the recommendation and comments.

  2. Click Confirm at the bottom. Show the follow-ups that appear: Thank Reviewer and Revert Decision.

  3. Open the three-dots menu on Rev Two to show Email Reviewer, Edit (change due dates), and Cancel/Unassign Reviewer.

Part 4, record a decision (TEST B):

  1. Show the four action buttons: Request Revisions, Accept Submission, Create New Review Round, Decline Submission.

  2. Click Request Revisions. Choose whether the revision needs a new review round, edit the author email, optionally notify reviewers, and click Record Decision.

  3. Switch to the author browser. Show Ana’s view of the revision request and where she uploads the revised file.

  4. Back as Ed Itor, show the revised file under Revisions. Explain the next move is a new round, or Accept Submission, which sends it to Copyediting.

Tip

Say this: “Open a new review round only after the author uploads revisions. Otherwise the author may upload into the wrong round.”

Block 4 (35–45 min): Copyediting and Production¶

The point to land: Copyediting turns a draft file into a copyedited file; Production turns that into galleys, checks the metadata, and schedules the article into an issue. Move briskly; most journals have one person doing both.

Copyediting (TEST C, about 4 minutes):

  1. Open the All in copyediting view and open TEST C.

  2. Show the two file panels: Draft Files (what came out of review) and Copyedited Files (the finished version).

  3. Point out Cory Copy in Participants. Assigning works exactly like assigning an editor: Assign, role Copyeditor, Search, OK. Assigning opens a discussion with the request.

  4. In Copyedited Files, click Upload/Select Files. Show the three options: upload a new file, select the draft unchanged, or select a file from a discussion. Check the file and click OK.

  5. Click Send to Production, keep the author email, confirm the copyedited files are selected, and click Record Decision.

Production (TEST D, about 6 minutes):

  1. Open TEST D from All in production. Show Production Ready Files and Lee Layout in Participants.

  2. In the left Publication menu, open Galleys > Add galley. Label it PDF, choose the language, click Save, upload the PDF, and click Complete.

  3. Click Preview (top right) to show the article page as readers will see it.

  4. Walk the rest of the Publication menu in one pass: Title & Abstract, Contributors, Metadata, References, JATS XML (new in 3.5, if your journals publish XML), Permissions & Disclosure, Issue. Remind people to click Save on each page.

  5. DOIs: if the journal uses Crossref, show the DOIs item in the left sidebar. Filter by Needs DOI, check TEST D, and use Bulk Actions > Assign DOIs. Mention that Deposit is a separate step (Bulk Actions > Deposit DOIs) unless automatic depositing is on.

  6. Click Schedule for Publication, choose the future issue you created in prep, and click Save. TEST D now shows Scheduled on the dashboard.

  7. Close the loop: in the left sidebar, open Issues > Future Issues, click the blue arrow on that issue, and show Preview and Publish Issue. Do not click Publish Issue on a live journal.

Tip

Say this: “Once an article is published, small fixes mean Unpublish, fix, reschedule. Substantive changes, like a correction or retraction, mean Create New Version. And OJS won’t push metadata changes to Crossref on its own; you redeposit.”

Block 5 (45–55 min): Scenarios¶

Read each scenario aloud and have attendees say (or, in a hands-on room, click) where they would go. Aim for 2 minutes each; skip the last ones if time runs short.

1. A reviewer hasn’t answered the request, and it’s past the response date.

On the dashboard you’ll see an outlined red circle. Click it to send a reminder or adjust the due date, or use the reviewer’s three-dots menu > Unassign Reviewer, then add someone else.

2. A reviewer declined.

Pink circle with a slash. Open the submission > Reviewers > Add Reviewer and assign a replacement. To try the same person again, use Reinstate Reviewer; they must accept the new invitation.

3. You declined a submission by mistake.

Open the Declined view (Journal Manager level), open the submission, and click Revert Decline. It returns to its previous stage.

4. The author replied to the OJS email from Outlook, and you can’t find the message in OJS.

Inbox replies aren’t logged. Ask the author to reply in the Discussions panel, or paste the reply into a discussion yourself so the record is complete.

5. An editorial or book review doesn’t need peer review.

In the Submission stage, use Accept and Skip Review.

6. A typo in a published article’s abstract.

Publication menu > Unpublish, fix it, Save, then Schedule for Publication again. If it has a DOI, redeposit the metadata from the DOIs page.

7. Which submissions have stalled?

Any dashboard view > Filters > Days Since Last Activity. Editors also get a weekly email listing copyediting and production items inactive for 30 or more days.

8. A colleague needs to join the editorial team.

Settings > Users & Roles > Invite to a Role. They must accept the emailed invitation before you can assign them. If they never receive it, the fix goes to the Site Administrator (TDL).

Block 6 (55–60 min): Wrap-up¶

End on where to get help, not on a recap.

  1. Put the lifecycle diagram back up and ask: “Which stage do you expect to spend the most time in?” Take one or two answers.

  2. Share the leave-behinds (paste these in the chat or the follow-up email):

    • Learning OJS 3.5: The Editorial Workflow, for editors, copyeditors, and layout editors

    • Learning OJS 3.5 for Reviewers and for Authors, to forward to their reviewers and authors

    • About OJS 3.5, including what changed in 3.5

    • The session recording

  3. Tell them who to contact: the library for workflow and journal setup questions; TDL (through the library) for account, email, and invitation problems.

  4. Take questions until time.

After the session:

  • ☐ Delete or decline the TEST submissions, or ask TDL to remove the test journal.

  • ☐ Send the follow-up email with links and the recording.

  • ☐ Note the questions you couldn’t answer; they’re your FAQ for next time.
